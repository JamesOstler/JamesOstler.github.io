Teaching

Students learn strategy by making consequential decisions.

My courses join analytical rigor to experiential learning. Students practice on models and cases, commit to recommendations under uncertainty, engage real organizations and audiences, and reflect on what should transfer to the next problem.

Two teaching platforms

Teaching Platform 1

Strategy, Competition, and Investment Judgment

How can students diagnose the game they are in and make defensible choices when outcomes remain uncertain?

The curriculum connects industry structure, competitive position, bargaining, value capture, cost and demand dynamics, options, information, organizational execution, valuation, and capital allocation.

Learning environments: economics of strategy, corporate and business strategy, angel investing, student investment funds, and investing through a liberal-arts lens.

Teaching Platform 2

Institutions, Policy, and Cross-Sector System Change

How can students understand why undesirable systems persist and design interventions that can survive implementation?

The curriculum follows rules and institutions through organizational incentives, intervention and coalition design, implementation, verification, accountability, and durable system change.

Learning environments: PACE, Strategy and Public Policy, LEAP legislative appraisal, and the Global Problem Lab.

Pedagogical method

Experiential, analytical, and reflective learning

Students do not merely apply a framework after an answer is known. They make the representation explicit, state assumptions, commit to a decision, encounter feedback from evidence or an authentic audience, and revise the reasoning—not just the conclusion.

1. Concept
2. Guided analysis
3. Decision under uncertainty
4. Action or recommendation
5. Feedback
6. Reflection and transfer

The method in practice

From bounded practice to public consequence

Skill builders

Students first work through focused analytical problems, explain the reasoning to peers, and transfer the method to a new context.

Live organizations and policy

Partner problems, legislative appraisals, and public showcases introduce real constraints, feedback, implementation questions, and accountable audiences.

Governed investment decisions

Portfolio journals, investment theses, angel evaluation, and student-managed funds preserve the decision record so reasoning and outcomes can both be examined.
