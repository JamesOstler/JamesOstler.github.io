Research Program 2 · From Knowledge to Action

The Firm as Shared Cognition

Jim Ostler

Working paper

StatusWorking paper
MethodFormal theory: Gaussian team theory and governance
Key constructCommonness as cross-member error correlation

The question

Why do firms pay to standardize employees on a shared AI artifact—and why would workers improve an artifact that may reduce the firm's dependence on them?

The central idea

An AI artifact has at least two distinct properties: how intelligent it is and how common it makes cognition across the organization. Greater commonness can improve coordination when work is interdependent. But it can also create correlated error, making the organization collectively fragile when the shared representation is wrong.

What the paper contributes

The paper makes commonness a design variable rather than treating it as an automatic consequence of adopting the same technology. It then connects the coordination value of a shared artifact to the governance of the worker investments that improve it. The firm must balance appropriation against the incentives required for employees to teach and develop the shared system.

Why it matters

Firms adopting the same general-purpose AI need not become alike. Advantage may depend on how the artifact is shared, where cognitive diversity is preserved, and whether governance encourages people to contribute knowledge without making their own position untenable.
