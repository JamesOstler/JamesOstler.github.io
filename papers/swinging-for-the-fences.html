Swinging for the Fences: Strategic Risk-Taking in Entrepreneurship

Revise and resubmit

StatusRevise and resubmit
MethodFormal theory
Key constructStrategic risk
ManuscriptAvailable on request.

The question

Why do entrepreneurs who are not dispositionally more risk-loving pursue high-variance strategies that incumbent firms avoid?

The central idea

Strategic risk is not the same as financial risk. In competitive settings where only a small share of entrants can win, increasing the variance of relative performance can improve an entrant's chance of crossing the threshold for survival or success. The same variance is unattractive to an advantaged incumbent trying to protect its position.

What the paper contributes

The paper reframes entrepreneurial risk-taking as a rational response to competitive structure rather than a psychological taste for danger. The value of variance depends on an actor's position in the competition: disadvantaged entrants may maximize it while advantaged incumbents minimize it.

Why it matters

Uncertainty does not merely threaten entrepreneurial action. Under some market structures, uncertainty is what makes entry viable. Policies or strategies that suppress variance may therefore protect incumbents even when they appear simply to reduce risk.

How it connects

The manuscript generalizes the strategic-risk mechanism developed empirically in The Competitive Dynamics of Strategic Risk-Taking, Unethical Behavior, and Entry. It is also the clearest bridge between the market-architecture program and the portfolio's cross-cutting lens of decision-making under uncertainty.
