Research Program 2 · From Knowledge to Action

Competing Representations

Nile W. Hatch and Jim Ostler

Working paper

StatusWorking paper
MethodFormal and computational analysis
Key constructCognitive exposure

The question

How do the pairings of rival firms' representations jointly determine competitive performance?

The central idea

A representation determines more than what a firm sees and how it decides. It also determines the firm's cognitive exposure: how strongly its performance depends on what its rival attends to, understands, or overlooks.

What the paper contributes

The paper moves managerial cognition from a firm-level attribute to a relationship between competitors. The value of a representation cannot be evaluated in isolation; it depends on the configuration created by both firms' ways of seeing the competitive environment.

Why it matters

Competitive advantage may come not only from seeing more accurately, but from seeing differently. Cognitive differentiation can change which moves are anticipated, which opportunities are contested, and which firms remain exposed to a rival's strengths.
