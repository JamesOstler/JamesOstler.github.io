Research

How architectures shape what actors can see, choose, and make possible.

My work examines the external rules through which markets form and the internal knowledge structures through which organizations act. Decision-making under uncertainty connects the two.

Research Program 1

From Regulation to Markets

How do rules become functioning markets—and why do some fail between policy intent and real outcomes?

Regulation creates categories, priorities, eligibility rules, and recognized forms of value. But those rules do not implement themselves. Organizations build and finance the systems that make recognition usable; market participants adapt to, exploit, or challenge the rules; and the resulting market may select very different actors than policymakers intended.

Research Program 2

From Knowledge to Action

How do organizations turn representations, records, culture, and shared knowledge into coordinated capability?

Organizations do more than process information. They shape what people attend to, how performance is recorded, which knowledge is shared, and what becomes common enough to support joint action. These designs influence judgment, coordination, learning, adaptation, and the value organizations can create with artificial intelligence.

Across the portfolio

Uncertainty is shaped, not merely faced.

Rules affect what can be measured and rewarded. Representations affect which possibilities enter a decision. Competitive position changes the value of variance. Organizational systems determine which uncertainty is reduced, shared, preserved, or strategically exploited.
